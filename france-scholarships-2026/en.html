If you are looking for scholarships to study in France in 2026-2027, your timing is good. The Eiffel Excellence call for 2027 opened on 2 October 2026, universities are setting their internal Eiffel deadlines for November, and most Erasmus Mundus programmes recruit between October and January. Two changes this year affect every non-EU budget: the student-visa financial minimum rose to €877.50 a month on 1 August 2026, and universities can now waive international tuition fees for fewer students. Below: what each programme pays, which ones can be combined, and what to do month by month for a September 2027 start.

Scholarships to study in France: which one fits you?

Students who win funding rarely apply for everything. They pick two or three schemes that match their level and calendar, and do those properly. A quick map:

  • Strong master's candidate, 29 or under: Eiffel, through your French university.
  • Drawn to a multi-country programme: Erasmus Mundus.
  • Applying to Sciences Po: the Émile Boutmy scholarship.
  • PhD: a doctoral contract, CIFRE or an Eiffel PhD grant.
  • Everyone: the embassy programmes on your own country's Campus France website.

Eiffel Excellence Scholarship 2027: open now, but not to you directly

Eiffel is the French foreign ministry's flagship award, managed by Campus France. The 2027 rules confirm €1,200 a month for master's students and €2,000 a month for PhD students. Campus France also pays your international and domestic travel, health cover (social security plus a free mutuelle), housing assistance and cultural activities. Programme fees are not covered, but as a French government scholar you are exempt from registration fees for national degrees at public institutions. The grant lasts 12 months for an M2, 24 for an M1, 36 for an engineering degree, and 12, 18 or 36 months for a PhD.

Candidates must be 29 or under (master's) or 35 or under (PhD) at selection, and apply in one of seven fields: biology and health, ecological transition, mathematics and digital sciences, engineering, French language and civilisation, law and political science, economics and management. Dual French nationals are excluded, and so are master's candidates already studying in France. An MSc qualifies only with the Conférence des Grandes Écoles label; distance and apprenticeship programmes don't. Two rules catch people every year: only one institution may nominate you (two nominations means rejection), and if you were turned down in an earlier Eiffel round at the same level, you cannot be nominated again.

Note: the 2027 application guide still quotes the 2026 birth-date cut-offs (after 31 March 1997 for master's, 31 March 1991 for PhD). Ask your university which date applies.

The dates that matter

Universities must file with Campus France by 6 January 2027; results are e-mailed in the week of 12 April 2027. Your own deadline is much earlier: 15 November 2026 at Institut Agro Dijon and EURECOM, and 6 November 2026 for PhD files at Université Paris-Saclay (cotutelle projects only this year). Most schools want your admission file in first.

How files are scored

The master's grid scores 40 points. Thirty assess you: 18 for your record and activities, 5 for the coherence of your path, 4 for your project, 3 for being recruited from your home country. Ten assess the host institution, including what it adds itself, such as funding, a reserved room or tutoring. A university that offers you a fee waiver also raises your Eiffel score.

University excellence scholarships: Paris-Saclay, IP Paris, Sciences Po

Many research universities fund their own awards. Usually the programme director shortlists admitted students, so an early, strong admission file is what decides it.

Université Paris-Saclay pays €10,000 a year, monthly from September to June, plus up to €900 for travel and visa, renewable from M1 to M2. You must be enrolling in France for the first time and be under 30; other funding above €600 a month disqualifies you, and it cannot be combined with Eiffel or Erasmus Mundus. Payments start only after arrival. In 2026 applications closed on 31 March; 2027 dates are not yet published.

Institut Polytechnique de Paris considers PhD Track admits automatically. Its Full Package combines a €10,000-a-year master's scholarship with three years of doctoral funding (about €82,800 gross).

Sciences Po's Émile Boutmy scholarship cuts tuition: a full waiver or €9,500 for the bachelor's, €18,500 a year for the master's. It targets first-time applicants from outside the EU whose household does not pay taxes in the EU; you request it in the "Financial information" section. Master's deadlines for 2027 entry are 18 October and 29 November 2026; bachelor's, 13 January 2027. Deferring your place cancels it.

Erasmus Mundus Joint Masters

Erasmus Mundus programmes are run by universities in several countries, often including a French one. The 2026 Programme Guide sets the scholarship at €1,400 a month for the whole programme (up to 24 months), with no tuition fees for scholarship holders. Everyone can apply, but no more than 10% of a programme's scholarships may go to one nationality, so your real competition is often your compatriots. You can hold only one in your life. Applications go directly to each programme, mostly October to January; start with the official catalogue.

French embassy and Campus France programmes in your country

This is the section most students skip, and often where their best chance lies. According to Campus France, only about a quarter of the foreign ministry's scholarships go through Eiffel and France Excellence Major; French embassies run the rest. Each designs its own scheme, so check your country's Campus France site (usually yourcountry.campusfrance.org), the embassy website and the Campus Bourses database.

Two examples show the pattern. India's Charpak master's scholarship pays €860 a month, is open to applicants aged 30 or under and disqualifies essays with significant AI-generated content. Haiti's 2026-2027 Master 2 call required admission via Études en France, an age under 35, and closed on 29 May 2026. Expect spring deadlines, priority fields and an interview. Graduates of French lycées abroad should also look at AEFE's France Excellence Major (about 200 new scholars a year, bac with "mention très bien" required).

Regional and foundation grants

Regions, cities and foundations fund grants too, but mostly through universities or exchange agreements, not open calls. EURECOM, for example, offers an excellence award of up to €9,000. Filter Campus Bourses by "Regions" or "Foundations", then ask each school's international office what it adds. Never pay for a "secret list" of scholarships.

Funded PhDs: doctoral contracts and CIFRE

In France, a funded PhD is a job. A doctoral contract lasts three years, has no age limit and has paid at least €2,300 gross a month since January 2026; doctoral schools recruit, so find a supervisor and topic first. With CIFRE, a company hires you for at least €27,600 gross a year and ANRT pays it €14,000 a year. There is no nationality condition, health disciplines are excluded, and ANRT decides within three months. European MSCA networks also recruit internationally via Euraxess.

If your lab is a restricted zone (ZRR), you need an access authorisation before you sign or enrol. The ministry has two months; silence counts as refusal. Ask your supervisor about this early.

Tuition fees and fee waivers in 2026-2027

Non-EU students at public universities pay €2,902 a year for a licence and €3,950 for a master's (EU students: €178 and €255). A doctorate costs €398 for everyone, plus the €105 CVEC contribution. A decree of 19 May 2026 caps fee waivers at 30% of a university's international students in 2026-2027, then 25% in 2027-2028. Ask each university in writing whether it grants waivers and when it decides, before your budget depends on one.

CROUS housing and grants for international students

A common myth first: the needs-based CROUS grant is not for students arriving from abroad. It requires two years of residence and a tax household in France. CROUS rooms are different. International students apply in the complementary phase on trouverunlogement.lescrous.fr, which opened on 7 July in 2026. Plan for a month's deposit, a guarantor (Visale is accepted) and home insurance, and keep a private plan B; our housing assistance can help.

Scholarships and the student-visa financial minimum

Since a decree of 22 June 2026, you must show monthly resources of 47% of the gross monthly minimum wage (SMIC) on the day you apply: €877.50 a month, or €10,530 a year, when it took effect on 1 August 2026, up from €615. It now follows the SMIC, so recheck it before applying in 2027. A scholarship certificate counts as proof: Eiffel and Erasmus Mundus clear the bar alone, while partial grants may need a top-up. The visa costs €50 in Études en France countries, €99 elsewhere. See how much money you need for a French student visa and try our cost calculator.

How to stack funding without breaking the rules

Eiffel cannot be held with any other French state-funded scholarship. Paris-Saclay's award excludes Eiffel, Erasmus Mundus and other funding above €600 a month. What works: Eiffel plus Sciences Po's Boutmy, which Sciences Po explicitly allows, giving €1,200 a month plus €18,500 off tuition. Eiffel plus your university's fee waiver or reserved room. A university scholarship plus a CROUS room and a part-time job within the legal 964 hours a year. When in doubt, e-mail the scholarship office and keep the reply.

Timeline for a September 2027 start

  1. October 2026: shortlist programmes, ask each school its Eiffel deadline, book your language test. Sciences Po master's round one closes 18 October.
  2. November: Eiffel internal deadlines, Sciences Po round two (29 November), first Erasmus Mundus files.
  3. December to January: Études en France deadlines in many countries, most Erasmus Mundus deadlines, Eiffel files due at Campus France on 6 January.
  4. February to March 2027: university and embassy scholarship windows.
  5. April to May: Eiffel results (week of 12 April), university results.
  6. June to August: visa application, CROUS complementary phase.
  7. September: arrival, registration and a French bank account for your stipend.

Only the Eiffel and Sciences Po dates are confirmed for 2027; the rest follow the 2026 calendar.

Why good applications get rejected

The same reasons come back every year. Wrong door: writing to Campus France for Eiffel, two nominations, reapplying after a rejection. Bad timing: discovering the November deadline in December. Weak substance: an unexplained gap year, a project that doesn't link your degree to a real job, transcripts without a grading scale. And on the visa side, a funding plan that doesn't reach the minimum every month.

Your short checklist

  • Passport valid for the whole first year
  • Transcripts and diplomas with official translations
  • Language certificate at the required level
  • CV and a letter written for each programme (see our cover letter guide)
  • Two briefed referees
  • A month-by-month budget above the visa minimum
  • Your Campus France file, if your country uses it
